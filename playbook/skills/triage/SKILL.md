Adapted from the `triage` skill of `mattpocock-skills` (MIT, see the plugin's LICENSE).

Triage the Ticket the first message names, such as `T42`, in the repository you are in. You only read and propose: the User decides. Everything about the Ticket comes from the Tandembike tools; everything about the repository comes from its own instructions (`CLAUDE.md` or `AGENTS.md`, if it has them), its glossary and decision records, and its code.

## Roles

Two category roles, as labels: `bug` (something is broken) and `enhancement` (new feature or improvement).

Five state roles, as labels:

- `needs-triage`: the User needs to evaluate it.
- `needs-info`: waiting on the reporter for more information.
- `ready-for-agent`: fully specified, ready for an agent.
- `ready-for-human`: needs a human to implement it.
- `wontfix`: will not be actioned.

A triaged Ticket carries one category and one state. Approving a proposal sets them and swaps the old state label.

## Steps

1. **Read the Ticket.** Call `get_ticket` with the number: description, labels, acceptance criteria and every comment. Read earlier triage comments so you do not ask again what is answered. Use `find_tickets` to look for a duplicate or related Ticket.

2. **Check the codebase.** Learn the repository's domain language and decisions, then search for an existing implementation of the requested behaviour by domain concept, not only by the request's wording. For a bug, try to reproduce it from the reporter's steps. Note where you looked, whether or not you found anything.

3. **Propose.** Call `propose_triage` once with the Ticket's number, your category and state, and your reasoning in Markdown. Tandembike posts the reasoning as the Ticket's triage comment with the AI disclaimer (`> *This was generated by AI during triage.*`) at its top, so do not write the disclaimer and do not also call `add_note`. In the reasoning give:
   - the category and state you propose, with your reasoning;
   - where you looked in the codebase and what you found, including whether the behaviour already exists (then `wontfix`, pointing to where it lives);
   - for `ready-for-agent` or `ready-for-human`, a short brief: the current behaviour, the desired behaviour, the interfaces involved, what is out of scope. Describe behaviour, not file paths or line numbers, which go stale.
   - for `needs-info`, the specific, answerable questions, under "What we still need". "Please provide more info" is not a question.

   Put acceptance criteria in `acceptanceCriteria`, one line each, not in the reasoning. Leave it out to keep the Ticket's own. On approval they replace the Ticket's.

4. **Apply nothing yourself.** The proposal is all you write: the User approves or rejects it, and only an approval sets the labels and criteria. Never propose `ready-for-agent` for work you could not specify; the User makes that call when they approve. Do not call `update_ticket` to change labels.

End your final message with one line: the category and state you proposed.

## When you cannot finish

Say what stopped you in a short comment with `add_note`, starting with `> *This was generated by AI during triage.*`, and end with that. Do not propose what you cannot support.
